<p><strong>A Brief History of Telok Ayer</strong>
</p>
<p>Telok Ayer Street located just outside the HDG and beside the Amoy Street
Food Centre, is home to several historically significant sites and National
Monuments.</p>
<p>Telok Ayer is “water bay” in Malay and as the name suggests, used to face
the sea (now Shenton Way) and served as a landing site for early migrants
in 1800s. It was designated a Chinese district in 1822 by the founder of
colonial Singapore, Stamford Raffles and was prominent as a landing site
for migrants from South China and South India. Grateful for their safe
journeys, many would set up altars and shrines to thank their respective
gods.</p>
<p>Indian Muslims from the Coromandel Coast in south India, often referred
to as Chulias that had survived the perilous journey, built the <strong>Al-Abrar Mosque</strong> and
the <strong>Nagore Dargah Shrine (now Indian Muslim Heritage Centre)</strong> at
192 and 140 Telok Ayer Street respectively.</p>
<p>The Hokkiens from Fujian, China, set up a simple shrine in 1819, dedicated
to the Goddess of the Sea, Ma-Zu-Po to give thanks for their safe journey
from Southern China. The <strong>Thian Hock Keng Temple</strong> eventually
grew out of this shrine in 1842 and now houses shrines to several Chinese
deities.</p>
<p>Next to the Thian Hock Keng Temple is the Singapore <a href="https://taoism.org.sg/" rel="noopener nofollow" target="_blank">Yu Huang Gong</a>,
situated in a 180 year old building previously owned by a self help organisation
started by Peranakan Hokkiens from Singapore and Malacca. It is often mistaken
as part of the larger Thian Hock Keng Temple.</p>

<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="Telok Ayer Chinese Methodist Church" src="/images/Telok%20Ayer%20Methodist%20Church.jpg">
</div>
<p>The <strong><a href="https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/national-monuments/telok-ayer-chinese-methodist-church" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" target="_blank">Telok Ayer Chinese Methodist Church</a></strong> build
in 1925 could trace its history to a shophouse (no longer in existence)
at Upper Nanking Street in 1889. Its pioneering missionary Dr Benjamin
West preached in Malay which was then translated into Hokkien. Today it
holds services in Mandarin, Hokkien, English and Bahasa Indonesia.</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="Al-Abrar Mosque" src="/images/al%20abrar%20mosque%20.jpg">
</div>
<p>The <strong><a href="https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/national-monuments/al-abrar-mosque" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" target="_blank">Al-Abrar Mosque</a></strong> located
at 192 Telok Ayer Street was established in 1827 for the Tamil Muslim Chulias
from south India. The present building was built between 1850 and 1855
and it was also also known as Masjid Chulia or Chulia Mosque</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="Thian Hock Keng Entrance" src="/images/outdoor%20walkingtrail.jpg">
</div>
<p>One of the oldest Hokkien temples in Singapore, the <strong><a href="https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/national-monuments/thian-hock-keng" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" target="_blank">Thian Hock Keng Temple</a></strong> was
a humble joss house in 1821-22. It was erected in honour of Ma Zu Po, the
Protector of Sojourners, and Chinese immigrants would go there to offer
thanks for a safe sea passage from China . Between 1839 to 1842, the joss
house was rebuilt as Thian Hock Keng Temple by philanthropist Tan Tock
Seng.</p>
<p>Next to but distinct from the Thian Hock Keng Temple is the revamped
<a href="http://www.taoism.org.sg" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" target="_blank">Taoist Mission</a>or the Yu Huang Gong (Temple of the Heavenly Jade Emperor),
It is housed in the 180 year old Keng Teck Way Association building set
up by Hokkien Peranakan merchants from Singapore and Malacca.</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="Nagore Dargah Shrine" src="/images/nagore%20dargah%20shrine.jpg">
</div>
<p>The <strong><a href="https://www.roots.gov.sg/en/places/places-landing/Places/national-monuments/former-nagore-dargah" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" target="_blank">Nagore Dargah</a></strong> (Indian
Muslim Heritage Centre), previously named as the Nagore Dargah Shrine was
built between 1828 -1830 and was originally known as the Shahul Hamid Durgha.</p>
<p>The places of worship preceded the partitioning of the land in Telok Ayer
and construction of the shophouses in between, which used to be open ground.</p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="ACS House" src="/images/acs%20house.JPEG">
</div>
<p>The first Anglo-Chinese School in Singapore, which used to be located
at shophouse number 70, was started by Methodist missionaries with just
13 pupils along Amoy Street on 1 March 1886. Now called <strong><a href="https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/historic-sites/anglo-chinese-school" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" target="_blank">ACS House</a></strong>,
this place has been classified as a historic site since the late 1990s.</p>
<p>ACS although started by Methodist missionaries and Peranakan Chinese,
accepted multi racial students from different faiths.</p>
